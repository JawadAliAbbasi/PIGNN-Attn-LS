\section{What is being compared}

Four implementations, each trained one model per grid on the Phase-0 backbone
corpus: same parquet, same $1/3$--$1/3$--$1/3$ split, same seed 42. Both drivers
call \texttt{random\_split} with the same lengths and the same seeded generator,
so the held-out rows are identical and the numbers are directly comparable.

\paragraph{PIGNN is not the same kind of model, and the comparison is not
apples-to-apples.} The three GridFM variants are feed-forward: one pass, one
prediction, roughly 20 million parameters. PIGNN-Attn-LS runs $K=40$
physics-informed solver steps per scenario with 66 thousand parameters --- it is
closer to a learned Newton iteration than to a direct surrogate, and it pays for
that in inference cost. Its advantage below should be read as what the iterative
approach buys, not as one architecture being uniformly better.

\paragraph{Which grids are in the aggregate.} Win counts and medians quoted in
the text are over the \textbf{23 grids all four models have finished}, so the
four are always compared on the same set.

\paragraph{On residuals the gap is much larger than on voltage.} PIGNN takes
$\Delta P^{\infty}$ on 20 of 23 grids and $\Delta Q^{\infty}$ on 19. Median
$\Delta P^{\infty}$ is $1.29\times10^{-2}$ pu against graphkit's
$2.60\times10^{-1}$, mirror's $5.17\times10^{-1}$ and mirrorpin's
$5.59\times10^{-1}$ --- a factor of 20 against the best feed-forward model and
40 against the worst. This is the expected shape of the result: PIGNN's
iteration is driven by the power-balance residual itself, so it optimises
directly what this table measures.

\paragraph{graphkit is the best of the feed-forward three here.} It wins
$\Delta Q^{\infty}$ on 4 grids to mirror's 0 and mirrorpin's 0, and its medians
are roughly half theirs. Its \texttt{PhysicsDecoderPF} is doing something real,
but it closes a small part of the gap to an actual iterative solver.

\paragraph{PIGNN tracks the voltage profile essentially perfectly --- on the
grids it has been scored on.} Median PQ slope 0.999 and median $R^2$ 1.0000, and
it is the closest to slope~1 on 13 of the 15 grids where all four are available.
The other three sit at median slope 0.98--0.99 on the same 15.

\paragraph{That comparison is on the easy half of the corpus, and it is the
wrong half.} The 15 grids PIGNN has been scored on are the small ones, and none
of them is a grid where the feed-forward models collapse: the worst mirror PQ
slope among them is 0.396 (case89pegase), and every grid below that ---
case3120sp (0.19), LVN\_heo1 (0.21), case1888rte (0.23), case1354pegase (0.34),
case2869pegase (0.34), case9241pegase (0.49) --- is still missing a PIGNN score.
\textbf{The interesting question, whether PIGNN's iteration avoids the collapse
that afflicts half the corpus, is therefore still open.} The median comparison
above should not be read as answering it.

One data point leans the right way without settling it: case89pegase is the
hardest grid PIGNN has been scored on, and where mirror collapses to 0.396,
mirrorpin to 0.471 and graphkit to 0.696, PIGNN holds \textbf{0.830} with
$R^2 = 0.952$ against their 0.49--0.81. That is a single grid at the easy end of
the collapse range, so it is suggestive, not evidence.

\paragraph{How the PIGNN scores were validated.} The inference path had to be
established against the training logs rather than read off defaults, because
\texttt{GNSMsg\_EdgeSelfAttn} takes a dozen constructor arguments that change the
forward pass but are not stored in the checkpoint --- so a wrong value loads with
a perfectly matching \texttt{state\_dict} and silently computes a different
function. Three had to be corrected: \texttt{use\_armijo} (the decisive one),
\texttt{v\_limit}, and the missing \texttt{n\_nodes\_per\_graph} argument. With
\texttt{use\_armijo} off the model returned $|V|$ RMSE $2.05\times10^{-1}$
against a logged $8.76\times10^{-4}$, a factor of 234, with no error raised.

The 15 scores now reproduce their training runs to within \textbf{0.7\%
relative}, median 0.07\%. That is looser than the $2\times10^{-4}$ the GridFM
re-scores achieve, and the reason is structural: PIGNN runs 40 solver iterations
in float32, so ordinary GPU non-determinism compounds where a single forward pass
does not. It is agreement to three significant figures, not an unexplained
discrepancy.

\paragraph{The same trap applies to \texttt{predict\_pignn.py}} --- it omits
\texttt{n\_nodes\_per\_graph}, so figures taken from it should be re-checked
against a training log before use.
