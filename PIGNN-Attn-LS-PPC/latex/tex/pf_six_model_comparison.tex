\section{What is being compared}

Six surrogates, one model per grid, all trained on the Phase-0 backbone corpus
with the same parquet, the same $1/3$--$1/3$--$1/3$ split and seed 42. Every
driver builds its split with \texttt{random\_split} over the same lengths and
the same seeded generator, so the held-out rows are identical and the numbers
are directly comparable.

\paragraph{Coverage gaps, stated rather than hidden.} PIGNN ran on all 31 grids
but only 20 produced a usable model: on 11 the training driver's zero-grad guard
fired on 96--99.6\% of batches, so the optimiser step was a no-op and the
reported figures describe the initialisation. Those cells carry a
dagger$^\dagger$. GridSFM and LUMINA were re-run after the input defect below
was fixed and stand at 23 and 27 of 31; the rest are the largest grids, still
training. \textbf{A dash means not measured, never measured-as-zero.}

\paragraph{An input defect was found, fixed, and did not change the outcome.}
Both OPF drivers put the bus injection on a load node as
$\mathrm{clamp}(-S,\,\mathrm{min}=0)$ and gave a generator node only to slack
and PV buses. A PQ bus whose net injection is positive --- distributed
generation exceeding local load --- therefore reached the model as a zero load
and no generator, while the physics loss still scored the prediction against the
true $S$. On SimBench this deleted \textbf{99.7\% of the active-power magnitude
on PQ buses}; on case1354pegase 1.4\% of $P$ and 11.8\% of $Q$; on case118
nothing. The damage tracked how much distributed generation a grid carries.

The fix carries the injection signed and only on PQ buses, where no generator
node exists to double count it. It is verified by reconstructing the injection
from the graph the driver hands the model: 99.67\% of PQ active power
unrepresented before, \textbf{0.00\%} after (residual $2.9\times10^{-6}$~pu,
float32 rounding).

That is a useful negative result: it rules out the most obvious explanation and
says the cause lies elsewhere. It has not been identified. The voltage band was
checked and cleared --- the drivers default to $[0.5, 1.5]$ and the true
magnitudes stay inside it. What remains untested is whether 40 epochs at
LR $10^{-4}$, carried over from earlier SimBench/LVN work rather than tuned per
model on this corpus, is simply the wrong budget for these two architectures.

\paragraph{The aggregate set.} Win counts and medians quoted below are over the
\textbf{18 grids where all six produced a trained model}, so no model is
credited or penalised for a grid another one is missing.

\paragraph{Not a like-for-like architecture comparison.} The four feed-forward
models make one pass per scenario. PIGNN runs $K=40$ physics-informed solver
steps with 65{,}896 parameters --- closer to a learned Newton iteration --- and
pays for it at inference. GridSFM and LUMINA are OPF architectures asked to do
PF from scratch here, with hyperparameters that were not tuned for the task.

\paragraph{GridSFM and LUMINA do not transfer to power flow in this setting.}
They remain the two worst models on every metric family after the input fix.
Median angle $4.89^\circ$ (gridsfm) and $3.07^\circ$ (lumina) against
$0.40$--$0.49^\circ$ for the GridFM variants and $0.13^\circ$ for PIGNN. On mean
active residual they are worse than every GridFM variant by a factor of 2 to 13.

This contrasts with the OPF study, where both performed respectably. Three
differences are worth keeping in view before reading it as a verdict on the
models:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{They are trained from scratch here.} The OPF work had released
weights available; this does not. Whatever those architectures gain from
pretraining is absent.
\item \textbf{The hyperparameters are not theirs.} All six run 40 epochs at the
same loss weighting; LR $10^{-4}$ was carried over from the earlier SimBench/LVN
runs, not tuned per model on this corpus.
\item \textbf{The task is not the one they were designed for.} Both are OPF
surrogates. The PF adaptation is faithful to each schema --- verified: the
specified $|V|$ reaches the model, the unspecified reactive injection at PV and
slack is zeroed rather than leaked, and the slack active power in the parquet is
not the solved value, and after the fix the PQ-bus injection arrives intact ---
but the architecture still carries a generator/dispatch structure that PF does
not use.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{None of these three has been tested.} They are the candidates that
remain after the injection defect was ruled out, not conclusions.

\section{Caveats}

\paragraph{Single seed.} Every number is one run. The same configuration has
given $1.040\times10^{-3}$ and $4.300\times10^{-3}$ on case14 across clusters,
so differences below roughly a factor of two should not be read as real. That
threshold matters for mirror vs mirrorpin; it does not for PIGNN's residual
margins or for the gap to gridsfm/lumina.

\paragraph{GridSFM is 23/31 and LUMINA 27/31; the missing ones are the largest
grids.} Their aggregate therefore describes the small and mid-size regime, as
does PIGNN's. Nothing here supports a claim about any of them on large
transmission networks.

\paragraph{The eleven dead PIGNN runs need re-running.} The cause is identified:
\texttt{-{}-armijo\_mode} defaults to \texttt{fixed}, which discards the solver
step when neither the line search nor its fallback decreases the mismatch,
leaving the output equal to the constant input and the loss detached. Measured
directly: with \texttt{fixed} the output is not attached to the parameters and
the gradient norm is undefined; with \texttt{geometric} or
\texttt{geometric\_safe} it is attached at gradient norm $2.707\times10^{-1}$.

\paragraph{case145 is not comparable} on any row: 7885 scenarios against about
36{,}000 for the rest.

case6515rte 20{,}135, case6495rte 23{,}332, case6470rte 25{,}551, case300
