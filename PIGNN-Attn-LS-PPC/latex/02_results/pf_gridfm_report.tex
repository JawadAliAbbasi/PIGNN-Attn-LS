\section{Scope}

One AC power-flow surrogate trained per grid across the Phase-0 backbone corpus:
given the bus injections, predict the voltage state. This is the power-flow task
(\texttt{-{}-task pf}), not OPF --- no decision space, no dispatch, no cost.

\paragraph{Four campaigns.} All 31 grids were trained once per GridFM
implementation --- 93 runs, all finished --- and a fourth campaign trains
PIGNN-Attn-LS on the same data, 23 of 31 finished so far:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Tag & Model & $|V|$ at PV/slack, $\theta$ at slack & Status \\
\midrule
\texttt{mirror}    & \texttt{GridFMHeteroSurrogate} (local) & predicted & 31/31 \\
\texttt{mirrorpin} & the same, \texttt{-{}-pin\_known}      & held to the given input & 31/31 \\
\texttt{graphkit}  & IBM \texttt{GNS\_heterogeneous}        & held to the given input & 31/31 \\
\texttt{PIGNN}     & PIGNN-Attn-LS, unrolled solver         & predicted & 23/31 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\texttt{mirror} is a local re-implementation in GridFM's architectural style,
not IBM's released model. The released \texttt{GNS\_heterogeneous} adds
per-layer physics feedback and a \texttt{PhysicsDecoderPF}. Neither has
published weights, so all 93 runs are from scratch and none is fine-tuned;
this compares architectures, not pretraining. \textbf{Do not read the
\texttt{mirror} numbers as ``GridFM's'' performance.}

\texttt{mirrorpin} exists because \texttt{mirror} was found to violate the
problem statement --- see Section~\ref{sec:graphkit}. It is the variant to use
for new work; \texttt{mirror} is retained so the first campaign stays
reproducible.

\paragraph{Data.} The Phase-0 backbone corpus: the pandapower-solved scenario
set tagged \texttt{\_ppNR\_}, plus LVN\_heo1, which carries \texttt{\_cNR\_}
because pandapower cannot solve that grid and it was produced with the custom
Newton oracle. 31 grids, 4 to 9241 buses, mostly 36{,}000 scenarios each, split
$1/3$ train, $1/3$ valid, $1/3$ test.

\paragraph{Setup.} 40 epochs, AdamW at LR $5\times10^{-4}$, hidden 48, 12
layers, 8 heads (the released config's capacity), loss
$\mathrm{MSE}(V) + 10^{-2}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm phys}$ with a log-cosh physics
form. Per-unit on a 100~MVA base, complex128 throughout. Run on helma H100s,
one job per grid.

\section{Two changes the corpus forced}

\paragraph{Row groups.} Most \texttt{*\_NR\_branchrows\_directSI.parquet} files
were written with 3000--6000 rows per group. Training shuffles, so a batch of a
few samples decodes a whole group; the same effect measured 3191~ms/batch
against 176~ms/batch on the OPF files, an $18\times$ penalty. 28 files were
re-laid-out to 20 rows per group before training. SimBench, ENTSO-E and
LVN\_heo1 were already at 20.

\paragraph{A sparse Y-bus.} The collate built a \emph{dense} block-diagonal
Y-bus, costing $(B\!\cdot\!N)^2\times16$ bytes --- quadratic in batch times
buses. case2848rte at batch 32 asked for 124~GB and every large grid was
OOM-killed. The matrix is 99.98\% zeros, so it is now assembled as sparse COO:

With the Y-bus out of the way the binding constraint became the model's own
activation memory. Measured directly on case9241pegase --- 4.3~GB at batch 1

$\sim$479~KB per node and sets the per-grid batch (12 for case9241pegase, 64 for
everything up to case1354pegase).

\paragraph{Pinning does what it was meant to, and only that.} \texttt{mirrorpin}
improves $|V|$ on \textbf{28 of 31} grids, median ratio $0.83$, which is the
predicted consequence of no longer moving away from values it was handed. The
gain is concentrated where the pinned fraction is large: GBreducednetwork
$2.29\times10^{-2}\rightarrow5.09\times10^{-3}$ ($4.5\times$) and case5
$2.61\times10^{-3}\rightarrow4.82\times10^{-4}$ ($5.4\times$).

But combined RMSE improves on only 17 of 31 (median ratio $0.985$), because the
angle gets slightly \emph{worse} more often than not --- mirror wins the angle
on 16 grids to mirrorpin's 9. Pinning removes an error source at PV and slack
buses and does not touch the angle at PQ buses, so this is not a mechanical
consequence of the change; the most likely reading is that freeing the head from
having to fit the pinned magnitudes shifts capacity around, and a single seed
per grid cannot separate that from run-to-run noise. \textbf{The honest
statement is that pinning is a clear win on magnitude and neutral-to-slightly-negative
on angle.}

\paragraph{The released model is behind on voltage.} graphkit loses to
mirrorpin on combined RMSE on 24 of 31 grids, median ratio $1.50$, and the
losses are worst on the large rte/pegase grids --- case6470rte
$6.15\times10^{-2}\rightarrow1.77\times10^{-1}$, case2869pegase
$6.24\times10^{-2}\rightarrow2.01\times10^{-1}$, ENTSO\_E\_RealGridTest
$9.03\times10^{-3}\rightarrow8.01\times10^{-2}$ (a factor of 8.9). It wins
clearly on the smallest grids (case4gs, case5, case6ww, case9, case14), where
most buses are pinned. Section~\ref{sec:graphkit} separates how much of that is
the masking rather than the architecture.

\section{Results: power-balance residuals}

does not say whether that state satisfies the network equations, which is what
an operator would care about. The residual
$\Delta S = S_{\rm implied} - S_{\rm set}$ is computed from
$S = V\odot\overline{YV}$ and masked by bus type: active power is not scored at
the slack and reactive power is not scored at slack or PV buses, because there
the injection is a free variable rather than a known input.

\paragraph{The residual ranking reverses the voltage ranking.} graphkit is the
worst of the three on voltage and the best of the three on residual, by a wide
margin: median $\Delta P^{\infty}$ 1.43~pu against mirror's 5.52 and
mirrorpin's 7.77, and it wins $\Delta P^{\infty}$ on 19 of 31 grids and
$\Delta Q^{\infty}$ on 22 of 31. This is the clearest single result in the
report. The released model carries a \texttt{PhysicsDecoderPF} and per-layer
physics feedback, and the residual is exactly the quantity that machinery
targets --- so the architecture is doing what it was designed to do, at a cost
in state accuracy. Which of the two matters depends on the application, and
reporting either metric alone would have declared a different winner.

\paragraph{Pinning helps the voltage and hurts the reactive balance.}
\texttt{mirrorpin} is worse than \texttt{mirror} on $\Delta Q^{\infty}$ on
\textbf{22 of 31} grids and better on \emph{none} --- it wins 0 of 31 --- while
simultaneously improving $|V|$ on 28 of 31. The regressions are large:
LVN\_heo1 $23.4\rightarrow8417$~pu ($360\times$), case9241pegase
$53.5\rightarrow432$ ($8.1\times$), case2869pegase $17.7\rightarrow135$
($7.6\times$).

A plausible mechanism is that the unpinned model was using the PV-bus magnitudes
it was free to move as slack to absorb reactive mismatch, and pinning removes
that freedom without giving the model a better way to satisfy the balance.
\textbf{This is a hypothesis, not a measurement:} it predicts the effect should
scale with the pinned fraction, and LVN\_heo1 --- 99\% PQ, so almost nothing is
pinned --- is the worst regression of all, which the hypothesis does not
explain. LVN\_heo1's $\Delta Q^{\infty}$ of 8417~pu is also physically absurd
as a modelling error and is more likely a degenerate bus. Both need
investigation before the effect is described as understood.

The practical consequence: \textbf{reporting voltage RMSE alone would have rated
iceland (rank 20 of 30 in that table) as mid-pack when it has the worst mean active-power
residual in the corpus}, and would have understated LVN\_heo1, which is 16th on
voltage but 6th on residual.

\paragraph{The residual distribution is extremely heavy-tailed.} On two grids
the mean exceeds the 95th percentile --- iceland at
$\overline{|\Delta P|}=8.18$ against p95 $=0.899$, and GBnetwork at 4.39 against
3.98. That ordering is only possible if a small number of buses carry residuals
orders of magnitude above the rest. Reporting a mean alone would hide this
entirely, and reporting $\Delta P^{\infty}$ alone would suggest the whole grid is
bad when it is a handful of buses. Both are needed.

The worst $\Delta P^{\infty}$ values are physically implausible as
\emph{modelling} error: GBnetwork reaches 1300~pu, i.e.\ 130~GW at a single bus.
These are better read as a signal that something degenerate is happening at
specific buses --- most likely near-zero-impedance branches or isolated
components --- than as a smooth accuracy figure.

\paragraph{Absolute error is dominated by the angle.} Across the corpus
$|V|$ RMSE spans $1.0\times10^{-3}$ to $3.1\times10^{-2}$, a factor of 31, while
$\theta$ spans $0.034^\circ$ to $11.7^\circ$, a factor of 344, so the ranking is
set almost entirely by the angle. It does \emph{not} follow that magnitude is
the easier channel: Section~\ref{sec:diag} shows the magnitude is the one that
collapses toward its mean on half the corpus, which a small absolute error
conceals.

\paragraph{Bus count does not predict difficulty; grid family does.}
GBreducednetwork has 29 buses and a worse RMSE ($3.19\times10^{-2}$) than
case118 with 118 buses ($1.14\times10^{-2}$); case6495rte with 6495 buses beats
case300 with 300. What does track is provenance: the IEEE-standard cases occupy
the top of the table and the pegase / rte / sp / GB families the bottom,
independent of size. Whatever makes those grids hard --- ill-conditioning,
topology, voltage-level spread --- is not captured by bus count.


\section{MAE and the collapse diagnostic}
\label{sec:diag}

Sections 3 and 4 report RMSE and residuals. Two papers argue that is not enough,
and both turn out to matter here.

\paragraph{Why MAE alone is also not enough.} GridSFM's white paper makes the
point against its own headline metric: bus voltage magnitude in pu spans only
about 0.95--1.10, so \emph{a flat predictor of $V = 1.0$ at every bus already
posts a small MAE} while carrying no information about the voltage profile.
MAPE has the mirror problem, being dominated by the small-denominator tail.
Neither says whether a model follows the reference's variation or collapses
toward its mean. Their remedy, adopted here, is a per-channel pooled regression
of predicted against reference values: slope~1 means the model tracks the
reference spread one-for-one, and slope well below~1 means collapse. We regress
the angle on $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ rather than on $\theta$, because a
wrapped angle is circular and a raw fit is corrupted by any pair straddling the
branch cut.

This is not a hypothetical failure mode for this project. A GridSFM run on
OPFData case118 was found to have collapsed its magnitude head only because two
independently initialised runs reported a $|V|$ RMSE identical to five
significant figures. A slope near zero would have shown it directly, from one
run.

\paragraph{Read the PQ column, not the pooled one.} Two of the three
implementations copy $|V|$ through unchanged at PV and slack buses. At those
buses prediction equals reference exactly, which drags the pooled regression
toward slope~1 for a reason that has nothing to do with modelling. The
right-hand block restricts the regression to PQ buses, where all three
implementations are actually predicting, and it is the column to compare.

fall from 7 to 1. Counting PQ-restricted slopes, they go from 7 to \textbf{8}.
The pooled number is inflated by exactly the mechanism this section warns

regression. Median PQ slope barely moves at all, 0.891 (mirror) to 0.871
(mirrorpin). \textbf{Pinning rescues the catastrophic cases and does nothing for
the moderate ones.}

\paragraph{The released model collapses hardest.} graphkit has the worst
tracking of the three: median PQ slope 0.805, 19 of 31 below 0.9 and \textbf{10
below 0.5}. Two cases are pathological rather than merely poor:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{case145 has a negative slope}, $-0.395$ pooled and $-0.199$ on PQ
buses. The prediction is \emph{anti}-correlated with the reference: where the
solver says the voltage is high, the model tends to say it is low. Its MAE
($1.06\times10^{-1}$) flags it as bad, but only the sign of the slope shows that
the model has learned an inverted relationship rather than a weak one.
\item \textbf{case6495rte (0.146) and case6515rte (0.187)} track almost none of
the profile, while the same grids under mirror sit at 0.831 and 0.684 and under
mirrorpin at 0.855 and 0.831.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{One grid fails in the opposite direction.} GBreducednetwork under
mirror has a $|V|$ slope of \textbf{2.428} with $R^2 = 0.283$: it produces
roughly two and a half times the reference spread, and that excess is largely
uncorrelated with the reference. Amplitude inflation with low explained variance
is a different pathology from collapse, and only the slope/$R^2$ pair
distinguishes them --- a variance ratio alone would call this grid healthy.
This grid is also the sharpest illustration of why the pooled column must not be
trusted. Pinning appears to fix it --- the pooled slope falls from 2.428 to a
healthy-looking 0.889 --- but on PQ buses mirrorpin sits at \textbf{0.391} with
$R^2 = 0.030$, which is worse than mirror's 1.139, not better. At 17\% PQ this
is the most heavily pinned grid in the corpus, so the pooled figure is dominated
by buses the model no longer predicts. Nothing was fixed; the defect moved out
of view. graphkit is the only implementation that actually handles this grid,
at PQ slope 0.943, $R^2 = 0.948$ and MAE $1.5\times10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{This corrects the earlier reading.} Section~5 concluded that ``error
lives in the angle, not the magnitude'', on the grounds that $\theta$ error
spans a far wider range than $|V|$ error. The regression says the opposite about
\emph{tracking}: the angle channel is healthy nearly everywhere
($\sin\theta$ slope median 0.992), while the magnitude channel is what
collapses. Both statements are true and they are about different things --- the
angle carries more absolute error but follows the reference, whereas the
magnitude carries less absolute error while not following it. The magnitude is
the weaker channel, and RMSE alone hid that.

\section{The released model, and a defect the comparison exposed}
\label{sec:graphkit}

\paragraph{They are not solving the same problem.} In AC power flow $|V|$ is an
\emph{input} at PV and slack buses and the angle is an input at the slack; only
the rest is unknown. The released model masks accordingly --- it copies those
values through and predicts a correction only where the quantity is free. The
original mirror does not: its \texttt{forward} never receives the mask its own
feature builder computes, and it adds a correction at every bus.

\paragraph{On equal terms the released model does not win.} Restricted to PQ
buses, mirrorpin takes 16 of 31 grids, graphkit 12 and mirror 3. The median
mirrorpin/graphkit PQ MAE ratio is 0.845, i.e.\ the pinned mirror is typically

This overturns what the previous revision could see. With only the unpinned
mirror to compare against, graphkit led on 13 of 19 grids and the margin was
largest exactly where the pinned fraction was largest --- case5 (20\% PQ) by
$90\times$, case4gs (50\%) by $8.5\times$. That pattern was flagged then as
consistent with the advantage being the masking rather than the architecture,
and with mirrorpin available the comparison is now controlled and the flag was
right: the advantage largely disappears.

\paragraph{Where each one is genuinely better.} The remaining differences are
systematic rather than noise:

The split is almost exactly by grid size. Of the 15 grids with at most 118
buses, \textbf{graphkit wins 12}; of the 16 grids larger than that,
\textbf{mirrorpin wins 14}. The three exceptions to the first group go to
mirrorpin (case\_ieee30, case39) and mirror (SimBench); the two to the second go
to mirror (GBnetwork, case300).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{graphkit owns the small grids.} On GBreducednetwork it is not
close --- PQ MAE $8.8\times10^{-4}$ against mirrorpin's $1.0\times10^{-2}$, a
factor of 12 --- and it is the only implementation that tracks that grid at all
(Section~\ref{sec:diag}).
\item \textbf{mirrorpin owns the large transmission grids:} case6470rte,
case6495rte, case6515rte, case9241pegase, case1888rte, case2848rte,
case2869pegase, case3120sp, case1354pegase, ENTSO\_E\_RealGridTest and others.
On several of these graphkit is not merely behind but collapsed --- case6495rte
PQ slope 0.146, case6515rte 0.187.
\item \textbf{mirror wins 3 grids of 31.} There is no reason to prefer it over
mirrorpin for new work.
\end{itemize}

A size-dependent crossover is a more useful result than an overall winner would
have been, and it is invisible in any single aggregate. It also fits the
residual picture: the released model's physics machinery constrains the solution
usefully when the grid is small enough for the balance equations to pin the
state down, and appears to become a liability at scale.

\paragraph{The residual trade-off stands.} None of this changes Section~4:
graphkit remains far ahead on power-balance residuals (median
$\Delta P^{\infty}$ 1.43~pu against mirror's 5.52 and mirrorpin's 7.77, winning
19 of 31 on $\Delta P^{\infty}$ and 22 of 31 on $\Delta Q^{\infty}$). The
overall picture is that the released model buys physical consistency at the cost
of state accuracy and of voltage-profile tracking, and the pinned mirror makes
the opposite trade. Which is preferable is an application question, not a
benchmark question.

\section{Four-model comparison, all metric families}
\label{sec:fourmodel}

Sections 3--7 compare the three GridFM implementations. This section adds
\textbf{PIGNN-Attn-LS} (\texttt{GNSMsg\_EdgeSelfAttn}) as a fourth and repeats
every metric family over all four.

\paragraph{It is not a like-for-like architecture comparison.} The three GridFM
variants are feed-forward: one pass, one prediction, roughly 20 million
parameters. PIGNN runs $K=40$ physics-informed solver steps per scenario with
65{,}896 parameters --- closer to a learned Newton iteration than to a direct
surrogate, and correspondingly more expensive at inference. All four share the
parquet, the $1/3$--$1/3$--$1/3$ split and seed 42; both drivers call
\texttt{random\_split} with the same lengths and the same seeded generator, so
the held-out rows are identical.

\paragraph{PIGNN setup.} $d=4$, $d_{\rm hi}=24$, 8 heads, 8 attention layers,
$K=40$, Armijo line search on. 40 epochs, AdamW at LR $10^{-4}$, the same
$\mathrm{MSE}(V) + 10^{-2}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm phys}$ log-cosh loss the GridFM runs
used, per-unit on 100~MVA, complex128, the same sparse Y-bus. Run on alex A40s,
one job per grid. At 65{,}896 parameters it is \textbf{304 times smaller} than
the mirror model.

results.}} On 11 of the 31 grids the training driver's zero-grad guard fired on

\paragraph{PIGNN tracks best overall but is not immune, and it fails on
different grids.} Over the 20, PIGNN has the best median PQ slope, 0.998 against
mirrorpin 0.972, mirror 0.962, graphkit 0.959, and is closest to slope~1 on 16
of 20. Grids below slope 0.9 are 7 for PIGNN against 7/7/8 --- the same count.
Below 0.5 it is 1 against 2/3/2.

Where it differs is \emph{which} grids fail. On the two where the GridFM
variants collapse hardest it is clearly better: case1354pegase 0.626 against
0.341/0.451/0.352, case89pegase 0.830 against 0.396/0.471/0.696. But it has a
failure of its own that none of them share --- \textbf{iceland at 0.207, where
mirror sits at 0.891} --- and case300 at 0.677 against mirror's 0.929. The
collapse is not a property of the feed-forward architecture that iteration
cures; each approach has its own hard grids.

\paragraph{Two pathologies only the slope separates.} graphkit reaches a
\emph{negative} slope on case145, $-0.199$: its prediction is anti-correlated
with the reference, which no error magnitude distinguishes from a merely weak
fit. And mirror on GBreducednetwork sits at 1.139 with $R^2 = 0.089$ --- it
produces more spread than the reference and the excess is uncorrelated with it,
which is amplitude inflation rather than collapse. PIGNN handles that grid at
0.999.


\section{Caveats}

\paragraph{case145 is not comparable.} It is the worst grid on voltage
(RMSE $2.06\times10^{-1}$, $\theta = 11.7^\circ$) and fourth worst on residual,
and it is also the only grid with 7885 scenarios instead of 36{,}000 --- 22\% of
the data everyone else got. A $\pm20\%$ regeneration is outstanding; the grid
should be re-run before its number is used for anything.

\paragraph{Scenario counts are not uniform.} Besides case145: case9241pegase
18{,}280, case6515rte 20{,}135, case6495rte 23{,}332, case6470rte 25{,}551,
case300 27{,}994, case1354pegase 32{,}627, GBnetwork 34{,}424, iceland 35{,}605.
Cross-grid comparisons carry this confound.

\paragraph{The PIGNN campaign is incomplete, and not randomly so.} 23 of 31
grids have finished and the eight outstanding ones are the eight largest. Every
PIGNN number in Section~\ref{sec:fourmodel} therefore describes the small and
mid-size regime only. Nothing in that section should be aggregated with the
GridFM tables into a corpus-wide statement.

\paragraph{Coverage is complete for GridFM.} All three GridFM campaigns are 31/31 trained and
31/31 re-scored: 93 training runs and 93 inference passes. Every table in this
report covers the full corpus under all three implementations.

The re-scoring is validated rather than assumed: it reads the same parquet and
reconstructs the same test split, so its RMSE must reproduce the training run's,
and \textbf{all 93 do}, to better than $2\times10^{-4}$ relative. This also
confirms the mirrorpin checkpoints are being rebuilt with pinning enabled --- a
mirrorpin checkpoint scored without \texttt{pin\_known} computes a different
function and would not have matched.

\paragraph{Single seed.} Every number here is one run. The same configuration
has given $1.040\times10^{-3}$ and $4.300\times10^{-3}$ on case14 across
clusters, and two GridSFM scratch runs swapped rank between clusters, so
differences below roughly a factor of two between implementations should not be
read as real. This matters most for the mirror-vs-mirrorpin comparison, where
the median combined-RMSE ratio is $0.985$ --- far inside the noise. The
\texttt{SEEDS} knob in the dispatcher implements the three-seed protocol PFDelta
uses; it has not been run.

\paragraph{No pretrained baseline exists.} \texttt{gridfm\_graphkit} ships no
weights, so GridFM is scratch-only under either implementation. Unlike LUMINA
and GridSFM in the OPF study, there is no ``pretrained'' variant to compare
against, and none of these runs should be described as fine-tuned.
