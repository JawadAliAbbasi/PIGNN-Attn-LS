\section{Scope and status}

Four PF surrogates, one model per grid, trained on the regenerated \textbf{ppNR
v2} corpus. v2 is selected by the \texttt{\_u0clean\_sgenpert\_pvqvstart\_}
configuration tag rather than the \texttt{\_ppNR\_} solver tag: several
generations now share the latter, so selecting on it would silently mix corpora.
All four share the parquet, the $1/3$--$1/3$--$1/3$ split and seed 42.

\paragraph{Placement.} Grids were assigned to hardware by size: the 10 smallest
to alex a40, case39--case\_illinois200 to alex2 a100, case300--case1888rte and
the nine largest to helma h100. Batch sizes come from measured per-bus-sample
activation cost (gridsfm $\sim$1040 KiB, lumina $\sim$145, graphkit $\sim$479),
scaled to each card.

\section{Two failure modes, both identified}

\paragraph{Ten LUMINA runs died on a missing file.} Every LUMINA job on the alex
tier failed at \texttt{make\_model} with \texttt{FileNotFoundError} on
\texttt{lumina\_ckpt/lumina\_config.json}. LUMINA reads its architecture from
the released config even when training from scratch, and the file existed only
under the b313dc11 account while the dispatcher built the path from
\texttt{\$HOME\_BASE}. Copying it to the iwi5295h account and resubmitting fixed
all ten; they now train normally.

\paragraph{Twelve runs hit the 24-hour wall clock.} All twelve are among the
five largest grids, where a single epoch costs 2900--6700 s:

The fix is the partition, not the model: helma's \texttt{preempt} partition
allows \textbf{48 h} on the same h100 hardware. Ten of the twelve fit 40 epochs
there at their measured speed and were resubmitted unchanged. The two
case9241pegase runs do not fit even in 48 h, so their epoch budget was cut to
what does --- \textbf{pignn to 30 and gridsfm to 24 epochs}. Those two therefore
carry a smaller training budget than every other cell in this report, and their
numbers should not be read as equal-budget comparisons. Preempt jobs can also be
evicted by higher-priority work, and the drivers have no resume, so an eviction
costs the run.

\section{PIGNN diverges on four grids}

On LVN\_heo1, case1888rte, GBnetwork and case2848rte the PIGNN run produced a
constant validation RMSE, and the cause is \emph{not} the v1 defect. The
zero-grad guard that killed 11 runs in v1 fired \textbf{zero times} here ---
\texttt{-{}-armijo\_mode geometric\_safe} kept the graph edge alive as intended.
What happens instead is divergence:

\begin{verbatim}
Epoch  0 | train loss 1.1116e+04  rmse 1.0297e+00  (dPinf 1.126e+05 pu)
Epoch 40 | train loss nan         rmse nan         (dPinf nan)
\end{verbatim}

The initial loss is already $1.1\times10^{4}$ with a worst-bus mismatch of
$1.1\times10^{5}$ pu, and the run reaches NaN. The reported validation RMSE is
constant because the best checkpoint never moves past epoch 0. These cells are
marked \textsc{nan} and excluded from the aggregates.

\textbf{This is a different failure from v1 and needs a different fix.} The v1
problem was no gradient; here the gradient exists and is too large.
\texttt{geometric\_safe} always applies a step, including one that increases the
residual, which plausibly contributes --- it is the trade-off noted when the
mode was chosen. Gradient clipping or a lower learning rate on these grids is
the obvious next experiment; neither has been run.

\section{Caveats}

\paragraph{Coverage is incomplete.} 22 of 31 grids have all four models. The
resubmitted wall-clock runs are still training, and one graphkit and one lumina
grid remain outstanding.

\paragraph{Two cells carry a smaller budget.} pignn and gridsfm on
case9241pegase run 30 and 24 epochs against 40 everywhere else.

\paragraph{Single seed.} Every number is one run. Differences below roughly a
factor of two should not be read as real; PIGNN's margins are far above that
threshold, the graphkit--gridsfm gap on $|V|$ is not.
