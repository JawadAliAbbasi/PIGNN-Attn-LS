\section{What this compares}

The scenario corpus was regenerated after defects were found in v1. This report
asks a narrow question: \emph{for each model, does training on v2 instead of v1
change its accuracy?} It is not a model ranking --- that is
\texttt{pf\_v2\_results} --- and the two corpora are not two seeds of the same
thing, so a difference here is a property of the data, not of luck.

\paragraph{What changed in v2.} Three things, per the dataset overview: the
start-point jitter was removed (\texttt{u0clean}), \texttt{net.sgen} is now
perturbed with the same global scale as load (\texttt{sgenpert}), and PV-bus $Q$
is taken from \texttt{u\_start} (\texttt{pvqvstart}). The row counts also became
trustworthy, since v1 counted attempts rather than solved rows.

\paragraph{Matching.} Each row pairs the same grid, same model, same
$1/3$--$1/3$--$1/3$ split and seed 42. Only grids where \emph{both} corpora
produced a usable run enter the aggregates. Runs that never learned --- the 11
v1 PIGNN runs whose gradient was severed, and the 4 v2 PIGNN runs that diverged
to NaN --- are excluded from both sides rather than counted as results.

\paragraph{For three of the four models the corpus is close to irrelevant.}
graphkit, gridsfm and lumina all sit within a few percent of ratio 1 on $|V|$,
and their grid-win counts (13--24 of 26--30) are near the coin-flip line. The
one consistent movement is on the angle and the mean residual, where all three
improve by 2--25\%. That is a real but small effect, and it is the direction the
dataset changes predict: removing the start-point jitter makes the target a
cleaner function of the inputs.

\paragraph{PIGNN is the exception, and it moves in both directions.} Its voltage
error improves substantially --- median $|V|$ RMSE $6.88\times10^{-4} \to
1.43\times10^{-4}$, a factor of 4.8, and the angle by 3.2 --- while its residual
metrics get \emph{worse}: $\Delta P^{\infty}$ ratio 1.93, better on only 7 of 20
grids. A model that fits the voltage state more closely while satisfying the
network equations less well is an unusual combination, and this report does not
explain it. One candidate is that v2's \texttt{pvqvstart} change alters the
reactive injection at PV buses, which is exactly the quantity the residual masks
treat as free; another is that the two are simply different operating points for
the same solver.

\paragraph{The ranking is unchanged.} On both corpora the order is
\texttt{pignn} $<$ \texttt{graphkit} $<$ \texttt{gridsfm} $<$ \texttt{lumina} on
voltage and on every residual family, with no crossing. Whatever v2 fixed, it
did not change which model wins --- which is worth stating plainly, because it
was a live possibility that v1's defects were the reason GridSFM and LUMINA
trailed.

\section{Caveats}

\paragraph{v2 coverage is still incomplete.} 22 of 31 grids have all four
models. Runs resubmitted after the 24-hour wall clock are still training, so
these aggregates will shift.

\paragraph{Two v2 cells carry a smaller epoch budget.} pignn and gridsfm on
case9241pegase run 30 and 24 epochs against 40 elsewhere, because 40 does not
fit even the 48-hour preempt limit at their measured per-epoch cost.

\paragraph{Single seed on both sides.} A per-grid v2/v1 ratio mixes the corpus
effect with run-to-run variation, and no seed replication exists to separate
them. The grid-win counts are reported alongside the medians for that reason: a
count near half is what pure noise produces.

\paragraph{The corpora differ in size.} v2 carries about 37{,}000 rows per grid
against v1's 36{,}000, and the v1 row-count field was unreliable. The split
fractions are identical, so the absolute number of training scenarios differs
slightly between the two sides of every comparison.
